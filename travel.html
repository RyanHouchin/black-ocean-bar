Method

  1. Set up. Pit the dates. Measure the honey into a small bowl. Line a tray with parchment. Get a big mixing bowl and a medium one.
  2. Toast the nibs. Dry skillet, medium heat, 30–60 seconds, shaking the pan, until they smell like brownies. Tip them out and set aside.
  3. Brown the butter hard. Melt 1 stick in a small pot over medium heat. It will foam, then crackle, then go quiet and turn deep amber — about 5–6 minutes. The silence matters: it means every drop of water has cooked out, and that is what makes these shelf-stable. Off the heat.
  4. Mash the dates. Fork the soft pitted dates into a rough paste. If they're firm, mince them fine first. Do not soak them — added water shortens shelf life.
  5. Finish the fat. Off the heat, stir the ghee, cashew butter, and date paste into the browned butter until evenly combined. Small date flecks are fine.
  6. Let it cool. Wait until the mix feels warm, not hot (~110F, about 10 minutes).
  7. Dissolve the shilajit into the bowl of honey. Stir until it fully disappears.
  8. Whisk the wet. In the big bowl: warm fat mix, honey, vanilla. Whisk one minute until glossy and thick. Nothing raw goes in — that's the point of this build.
  9. Whisk the dry. In the medium bowl: whey, collagen, cacao, lucuma, bee pollen, spirulina, cinnamon, espresso, salt. Break up every lump.
  10. Combine. Pour the dry into the wet in two additions, folding with a spatula until it becomes one thick, even, fudgy dough. Squeeze a bit — it should hold together like cookie dough.
  11. Fold in the nibs until evenly scattered.
  12. Roll. Scoop and roll into balls, ~32g each — weigh the first few, then trust your eye. Set them on the parchment tray.
  13. Coat. Roll every ball fully in cacao powder — on the road it keeps them from sticking together. Strawberry dust if you like, then press 3–4 Maldon flakes into each top.
  14. Set and pack. Freezer 10 minutes, fridge 1 hour. For the trip: pack cold into an airtight container with parchment between layers, and keep it out of the sun.

Notes

Why it travels
No raw egg, no fresh royal jelly, and the butter's water is cooked out. Fat + honey + dates is a shelf-stable matrix — same reason halva and fudge survive a pantry.
Heat limit
Safety isn't the ceiling — melting is. Above ~85F they soften and go greasy. Fine in a backpack, not in a hot car or direct sun. They firm right back up when cool.
What's missing
The yolks and royal jelly from the original both want refrigeration. Dates stand in for the yolks. If your trip has a cooler, just make the original.
Scale target
Each ball should read ~32g on a kitchen scale. That's a 1.5" truffle.
Too crumbly
Add ghee, 10g (2 tsp) at a time.
Too wet
Add whey, 15g (2 tbsp) at a time.
Dates
Soft Medjools mash straight with a fork. Firm ones: mince fine first. Never soak.
Shilajit
Water-soluble, not fat-soluble. Warm the jar, wet spoon, stir into the honey — it vanishes.
Toast the nibs
Unlocks volatile aromatics. Makes chocolate three-dimensional.
Espresso
Standard pastry technique. Amplifies cacao without adding coffee flavor.
Salt on top
Discrete crystals create sweet/salt/chocolate contrast. Never mix in.
Whey varies
Check your label: grams protein per gram powder. Adjust totals accordingly.
